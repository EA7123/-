# AI drama story development

Use when the user asks to create, adapt, diagnose, or rewrite a script or episode. A supplied script's locked facts and dialogue remain unchanged unless adaptation is authorized. Story quality is judged on the page and in a read-through; Shot Cards cannot repair a missing dramatic cause.

## Brief before drafting

Capture: audience and episode format if supplied; genre promise; premise; protagonist's concrete want and deeper need; opposing force; stakes; relationship pressure; series arc; episode start state and end state; locked canon; target runtime. Mark absent information as an assumption, not a fact. For an isolated scene, work at scene scope rather than inventing a series bible.

## Build the episode from decisions

Use a beat sheet before dialogue:

```text
episode_id / script_version:
opening situation and question:
protagonist goal:
obstacle and opposing choice:
inciting disruption:
escalations caused by prior choices:
turn or reversal:
cost or sacrifice:
payoff and changed relationship/state:
ending question or earned cliffhanger, if episodic:
```

For each scene, record `entry state -> want -> obstacle -> tactic -> turn -> exit state`. A scene should change knowledge, power, commitment, or risk. Check that a later beat follows from a prior choice or consequence; coincidence may start trouble but should not repeatedly solve it. An opening hook should introduce a concrete tension the episode can develop. A cliffhanger should follow from the episode's own conflict, not withhold arbitrary information.

## Characters and dialogue

Give each principal character a distinct objective, tactic, pressure point, and change of behavior. Check whether their choices drive the next scene. Write dialogue as action: what the speaker wants from the listener, what they conceal, and what changes when the line lands. Prefer a specific reaction or decision over a generic emotional label. Read lines aloud for voice distinction, natural phrasing, and performable duration. Keep silence, interruption, or overlap only when they change the interaction.

When the user asks for a rewrite, record which beats, lines, relationships, and timing are locked; identify the smallest scene or line range that causes the problem. Provide the revised version and a brief change record. Do not replace tested dialogue to make a shot fit; adjust staging or timing first and surface an irreconcilable conflict.

## Script review gate

Review the draft separately from its cinematic treatment:

- Can the viewer name the protagonist's immediate want and obstacle?
- Does each scene change the situation and cause the next meaningful beat?
- Does the protagonist make a consequential choice rather than merely receive events?
- Are character voices distinct when names are hidden?
- Is important information understandable without explanatory dialogue?
- Does the payoff answer or transform the opening question?
- Can the spoken lines and physical actions fit the target runtime in a read-through or animatic?

Record findings with scene and line references, proposed fixes, and a second read-through result. A filled beat sheet is a planning artifact, not proof of excellent writing. Without a draft and audience review, describe the script as untested rather than assigning a quality grade.

## Handoff to directing

Pass the approved script version, beat IDs, immutable dialogue where applicable, character goals, reveal rules, and timing constraints to Scene State and Shot Cards. Keep story revisions versioned so downstream prompts and footage can be traced to the correct script.

# Shot-language decisions

Choose a shot by its effect on story information and viewer distance.

## Framing

- Extreme wide / wide: geography, isolation, scale, group relationships. Use before spatially complex action or as a deliberate withholding device.
- Full / medium wide: body language, blocking, entrances, exchanges, and action clarity.
- Medium: dialogue with gesture and environment still present.
- Medium close / close-up: a meaningful change in thought, power, recognition, or intimacy—not automatic dialogue coverage.
- Extreme close-up / insert: decisive evidence or sensory detail. Establish ownership and location first unless confusion is intended.
- Over-the-shoulder: relationship and pressure; shoulder size and camera distance imply dominance or intimacy.
- POV / subjective: align knowledge or sensation with a character. Establish whose POV it is.

## Angle and height

- Eye level: observational neutrality and direct human relation.
- High/low angle: express spatial or power relations only when supported by blocking and context.
- Overhead: pattern, entrapment, geography, or detachment.
- Dutch angle: reserve for a specific rupture in equilibrium; avoid as generic tension decoration.

## Lens bands

- 18–28 mm: environment and spatial exaggeration; close use can distort faces.
- 32–40 mm: immersive proximity with readable surroundings.
- 50–65 mm: natural emphasis and controlled perspective.
- 75–100 mm: isolation, compression, observation, and intimate reactions.
- 100 mm+: surveillance, extreme compression, or distant detail.

Use bands rather than false precision unless a real camera package is specified. Maintain a dominant lens tendency per sequence and deviate for a reason.

## Movement

- Locked: forces attention onto performance, composition, or off-screen sound.
- Pan/tilt: discovers or follows while preserving position.
- Dolly/track: changes spatial relationship; moving closer is not automatically emotional emphasis.
- Lateral track: reveals layers, parallels movement, or maps geography.
- Crane/jib: changes hierarchy or reveals spatial context.
- Handheld: embodied instability or immediacy; specify intensity.
- Orbit: shows relational change or disorientation; use sparingly.
- Zoom: changes visual attention without changing perspective; useful for observation or stylized rupture.

For every move, state its trigger, path, end condition, and narrative purpose.

## Focus, blocking, and duration

Use depth and rack focus to transfer attention between established planes. Let entrances, crossings, sitting, standing, turning away, and prop handling modify power and composition. Estimate duration from action and comprehension: hold until the intended information lands, then cut on thought, movement, sound, or revelation.

## Coverage design

Start with the minimum editable set: geography/master, essential relationship angles, performance-critical reactions, story evidence, and transitions. Prefer a designed master plus selective coverage over automatic wide/medium/close coverage of every line.
